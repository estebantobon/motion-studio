# Mode: tutorial

Teaches one task in 30–90 seconds. Clarity beats spectacle here: the viewer should be able to follow along and finish the task. Keep the craft rules for polish, but slow down — a step has to stay on screen long enough to be read and understood.

## Inputs
Works best with real screenshots or a screen recording of each step in ASSETS. If there are none, ask for them; a tutorial with invented UI teaches the wrong thing. Without assets, offer a conceptual explainer instead (shapes and type describing the idea, no fake interface).

## Structure
1. **Title card (2–3s)**: what you'll be able to do by the end, in plain words. "Turn a screenshot into a 9:16 promo."
2. **Steps**: one step per shot, 3–6s each depending on how much there is to read.
   - Step number + a verb-first instruction of 3–8 words ("Paste the prompt into Claude Code").
   - The real screenshot or clip, pushed in to the exact region that matters. Dim or blur the rest.
   - A highlight on the click target: a ring, a cursor, or a box that draws on the beat.
   - Keep the step number visible in a corner so viewers know where they are.
3. **Result (3–5s)**: show the finished outcome — the actual output file, screen or video.
4. **Recap / CTA (2–4s)**: the steps as a short checklist, or where to get the resource. Hold still for the last 0.5–1s.

## Pacing and readability
- Use 100–110 BPM (or the track's tempo); move on bars, not every beat.
- Instruction text larger than in ads, on a solid or heavily blurred backing so it reads over busy UI.
- Commands and code are typed out verbatim from the source, in a monospace font, at a readable size. Never invent commands or outputs.
- Captions for any narration: burned in, max two lines, inside safe zones.

## Defaults
16:9 for YouTube/web, 9:16 for Shorts/Reels. 45s. Silent.
